#   * אנכי                 -- tipexa here, pashta in the p-trad
#   * אלהיך ... מבית עבדים  -- etnaxta/darga/tevir/merkha/tipexa/merkha/silluq here, closing the

# The transcription takes the m-trad side of both, over a contiguous run nothing had flagged.
# It is the Exodus appendix elyon's own signature repeated, and the taxton pages' mirrored: on the
# taxton pages the m-trad ran ON at עבדים (etnaxta, closing only at על־פני), here as on p. 350 the

# printed elyon's nine.
#
# HOW NARROW THAT VERDICT IS.  The two dt/elyon strands differ in EXACTLY those two regions and
# nowhere else in 164 tokens -- taken before any transcription existed, so with no stake in the
# outcome.  The other ~155 tokens are identical in both traditions, so the whole stretch from
# לא־יהיה to לרעך is evidence that this page follows dt/elyon and no evidence at all about which
# tradition.  The elyon identity itself rests on the independent evidence at לא תרצח, which this
# Decalogue makes a chanted verse of its own (silluq + sof pasuq), as all four elyon strands do
# and no taxton strand does -- the taxton runs לא תרצח ולא תנאף ולא תגנב together into one verse.

# THE TWO CORRECTIONS, both recorded in the JSON with what they replaced, both slips and neither
# an edition divergence:
#   * band 2 (5:6, the first commandment): a darga on אשר, dropped between the etnaxta of אלהיך
#     and the tevir of הוצאתיך.  Ben re-read the printed line against a zoom (zoom_line) and
#     confirmed the darga is on the page and had been missed: מונ את תביר -> מונ את דר תביר.  Every
#     manuscript strand has a darga on אשר -- all four, plus the printed taxton -- and only the two
#     printed-elyon strands read munax there instead, so the darga is squarely on-strand and its
#     absence was the anomaly; Ben's own ex/elyon transcription (p. 350 band 2) has it too.
#   * band 23 (5:18, the tenth commandment): a geresh on שדהו, first typed as a qadma.  Ben
#     re-read it and confirmed geresh -- the second and final geresh of the printed line, before
#     ועבדו: רביע קד מה -> רביע גר מה.  This is the slip that carries the clearer signature,
#     surviving against BOTH dt/elyon strands (qadma against geresh in each): all four Deuteronomy
#     strands, both traditions and both readings, have geresh on שדהו and none a qadma, so no
#     site could support the qadma and the difference could only be a mis-read.

# PASOLEG KINDS: "distinguished" on the edition's own authority -- the sixteenth ma'ala of its
# introduction (p. כז) prints the narrow-sense paseq as a HOLLOW bar throughout the Tanakh and
# reserves the SOLID bar for munax-legarmeh, so the kind is read off the glyph (see
# simtan_ex_taxton's header for the fuller account).
#
# SEVEN STROKES, the most of any Simanim Tanakh page so far -- the two taxton pages carried one
# each and the Exodus appendix elyon (p. 350) four.  They split TWO HOLLOW and FIVE SOLID:
#   * פסל   (token 16, band 3)  -- HOLLOW, narrow-sense paseq, before וכל־תמונה (pazer)
#   * בשמים (token 19, band 4)  -- HOLLOW, narrow-sense paseq, before ממעל (pazer)
#   * במים  (token 25, band 5)  -- SOLID, munax legarmeh; mun_leg mun rev (מתחת, then לארץ)
#   * צוך   (token 69, band 11) -- SOLID, munax legarmeh; mun_leg mun rev
#   * שבת   (token 79, band 12) -- SOLID, munax legarmeh; mun_leg mun rev
#   * היית  (token 101, band 15) -- SOLID, munax legarmeh; mun_leg mun rev
#   * למען  (token 126, band 18) -- SOLID, munax legarmeh; mun_leg mun rev (across the band 18/19 break)
# The contrast is NOT new to the repo, and this exact 2-and-5 split, at these same seven positions,
# is already on record in the sibling Simanim TIQQUN's Deuteronomy elyon (simtiq_dt_elyon) -- the
# same Decalogue reading in the other Feldheim edition, whose header also carries pasoleg_kinds:
# distinguished.  What is new is the count in the Tanakh, whose earlier pages reached at most four.
# The split is also grammatically coherent, which is evidence the review loop had no stake in:
# legarmeh is a light disjunctive that all but always precedes revia, and each SOLID stroke does
# (mun_leg mun rev), while neither HOLLOW stroke does (each precedes a pazer).  So the glyph-shape
# reading and the grammar agree, independently of the introduction's rule.
#
# THE SMALL WAVY MARK ABOVE SOME WORDS IS THE RAFE, not an accent, and is not transcribed -- see
# simtan_ex_taxton's header, which identifies it documentarily from the ninth ma'ala of
# the introduction (p. כב).  It occurs on this page as well.
#
# THE RENDER IN THE JSON TAKES THE WHOLE PAGE, no crop, the workflow doc's default.  Measuring a
# crop is worth it only to exclude a foreign column, which is the Simanim Tiqqun's two-column
# setting and nothing else; this edition sets one column, and its marginal verse numbers and
# sidenotes fall inside their own line's band and make no spurious ones.  The page gives one band
# per printed line.  Band numbers are read off the --debug overlay: bands 2-24 carry the Decalogue
# (band 2 = אנכי, band 24 = לרעך:), band 1 the running head and band 25 the ornament rule and the
# page number.
#
# Conventions in force for this file:
#   * One token per ACCENT.  Normally that is one per chanted word, a maqaf compound being one
#     chanted word; the exception is a word bearing more than one accent.  None occurs here.
#   * "-" is a maqaf binding two ACCENTS into one chanted word ("mun-mer"), "+" its simple-word
#     counterpart, "_" binds two MARKS into ONE accent ("mun_leg").  Only "_" occurs here.
#   * A postpositive/prepositive accent written twice on one word -- once at its fixed edge and
#     once as a stress helper -- is ONE accent, so ONE token.
#   * meteg is NOT transcribed: it is not a first-class mark of the accent system.
#   * A narrow-sense paseq is not an accent and is not a token; it is recorded as the aside
#     "[paseq]" after the token it follows.  Two occur here (פסל, בשמים).  Legarmeh IS an accent,
#     written "mun_leg" -- one token, not an aside; five occur here (במים, צוך, שבת, היית, למען).
#
