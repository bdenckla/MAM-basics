For right now we'll just investigate that Psalms 72:15 word individually". The verse-final chanted word of Psalms 72:15 is a lone atom, and the verse, as MAM-simple has it, is וִיחִ֗י וְיִתֶּן־לוֹ֮ מִזְּהַ֢ב שְׁ֫בָ֥א וְיִתְפַּלֵּ֣ל בַּעֲד֣וֹ תָמִ֑יד כׇּל־הַ֝יּ֗וֹם יְבָרְכֶֽנְהוּ׃

## 1. Summary: MAM and the Aleppo Codex have one U+05BD; the Leningrad Codex has a second, after the silluq

1. **MAM has one U+05BD, on the kaf, and so has the Aleppo Codex: established, the Codex from Ben's reading of it on 2026-09-10.** Ben read the word in mgketer.org's image of Psalm 72, which is on the Codex's leaf 253v: it has one stroke, under the kaf, which slants like a merkha but cannot plausibly be read as one. His crop is `aleppo/page-snips/253v-Ps72v15-yevarkhenhu.png`. mgketer.org's page for Psalms 72 is `https://www.mgketer.org/mikra/27/72/1/mg/106`. MAM's source (`in/mam-ws/D1-Psalms.json` line 2148) has יְבָרְﬞכֶֽנְהוּ in a `נוסח` template whose note gives the Aleppo Codex's form as יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהוּ, with a hataf under the resh where MAM has a sheva with a varika. MAM's introduction quotes the Aleppo Codex with the same form in its list of hatafs under non-guttural letters (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch5.mediawiki` line 737, under the heading at line 716). Both quotations are about the hataf, so neither was made to record the meteg, and both agree with MAM's text. MAM copies the meteg as the Aleppo Codex has it wherever that manuscript survives (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch3.mediawiki` line 1328), and this verse is on the Aleppo Codex's leaf 253v, which has Psalms 71:18–73:10 (MAM's index of the Aleppo Codex, `in/mam-ws-intro/index-aleppo.mediawiki` line 613).
2. **The Leningrad Codex has a stroke under the kaf and one under the he, as UXLC 3.9, WLC 4.22 and WLC 4.20 record: Ben's reading of the manuscript, 2026-09-10.** UXLC 3.9 records יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהֽוּ׃ with no note on the word (`in/UXLC-39/Psalms.xml` line 13314), and the two WLC conversions have the same codepoints (line 33165 of `out/wlc422-u/1verses_08_2cps.json`, line 33140 of `out/wlc420-u/1verses_08_2cps.json`). Ben read the word in an image of the Leningrad Codex's folio 380A (`uxlc/data/lci_augrecs.json`, the record at line 15070). Sefaria's image of the folio is `https://manuscripts.sefaria.org/leningrad-color/BIB_LENCDX_F380A.jpg`, and tanach.us's page for the verse is `https://tanach.us/Tanach.xml?Ps72:15`. The stroke under the he stands farther left and a little lower than he expected, but plainly belongs to this line and not to the line below. The word is in the early middle of line 3, where the estimator (`page_and_guesses` in `py/py_uxlc/my_uxlc_location.py`) said column 2, line 5.5. His crop is `leningrad/page-snips/380A-col2-line3-Ps72v15-yevarkhenhu.png`, and that directory's README records the estimate as a calibration point.
3. **mgketer's transcription of Mikra'ot Gedolot ha-Keter records one U+05BD, on the kaf: established.** Its form is יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהוּ (`C:/Users/BenDe/GitRepos/MAM-private/mgketer/out/D1-Psalms/mgketer-json-massaged/D1-Psalms_72.json` line 646).
4. **In MAM the stress is penultimate, so the Leningrad Codex's stroke on the he is after the silluq.** MAM's one U+05BD, on the kaf, is its silluq, and Phonetic MAM's `jta` is `y^.va.r^.!khen.hu`. Koren's stress is penultimate too (item 5), the Simanim Tanakh has its two marks where Koren has its (item 6), and the parallels in section 4 of `doc/meteg-after-silluq-job-4-12.md` have the same suffix, stressed on the syllable before it. The syllables are those of MAM's 1 Kings 7:37, לְכֻלָּֽהְנָֽה׃: a stressed closed syllable, then an open last syllable with a long vowel, which has the second U+05BD.
5. **Koren has a U+05BD on the bet and on the kaf, and none on the he: Ben's reading, 2026-09-10, on its page D1-Ps-038.** Ben's judgment the same day is that the one on the bet is highly unlikely to be a silluq, so in Koren the word has a meteg and then the silluq, and its stress is penultimate. Koren thus agrees with MAM on the stress, has a meteg on the bet that MAM lacks, and has nothing on the he, where UXLC and WLC record a U+05BD.
6. **The Simanim Tanakh has a U+05BD on the bet and on the kaf, none on the he, and a vocal simple sheva under the resh: Ben's reading, 2026-09-10, on its page 1053.** Its two marks are where Koren has its. Its vocal sheva under the resh stands where the Aleppo Codex as MAM quotes it, UXLC 3.9, WLC and mgketer have a hataf patah, and Ben takes it to follow from the edition's editorial policy rather than from a choice made for this word; `doc/simanim-tanakh-signs.md` records no policy on hatafs under non-guttural letters. MAM's policy does the same thing: its introduction says that a hataf under a non-guttural letter in the Aleppo Codex or the Leningrad Codex marks a vocal sheva, and that MAM prints in its place a special sign over the letter with the sheva (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch5.mediawiki` line 718). That sign is the varika of MAM's form here.
7. **The earlier screen listed this verse, and the post-stress-meteg survey does not treat it.** `doc/meteg-after-silluq-screen-against-uxlc-and-wlc.md` puts Psalms 72:15 in its class (v) against both UXLC 3.9 and WLC 4.22 (its section 4), with 1 Samuel 17:5, 1 Kings 14:14 (against UXLC 3.9 only) and Psalms 70:2. The survey's post-silluq page, `gh-pages/post-stress-meteg-post-silluq.html`, treats 1 Samuel 17:5 alone.
8. **Yeivin and Breuer say nothing about this word.** Of the two books only CoS cites Psalms 72:15, once, as an unpointed verse-division example (chapter 10 §4, `C10-S001.md` line 365 under `C:/Users/BenDe/GitRepos/MAM-private/masorah-books/books/cos/md-export-of-docx/`). The ga'aya the U+05BD on the he would be, Breuer's type j after a mafsik, is named for 1 Kings 7:37 alone (CoS chapter 8 §46 note [^81]).

## 4. How the figures were made

The scripts below are throwaway scripts, which are not tracked.

1. One spelled out this atom codepoint by codepoint in every source above and in Phonetic MAM.
2. One wrote this file from a template. It lifted every pointed form and the `jta` from the data; asserted that the note's and the introduction's quotations of the Aleppo Codex agree, that UXLC 3.9 and both WLC conversions have the same codepoints, and how many U+05BD each form has; and checked the result with `has_std_mark_order`.
3. One estimated the Leningrad Codex's column and line for the verse-final atom and built the Sefaria, tanach.us and mgketer.org links in section 1. The tracked `py/main_verse_links.py` builds links of the same kinds.
