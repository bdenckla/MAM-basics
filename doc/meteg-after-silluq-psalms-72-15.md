For right now we'll just investigate that Psalms 72:15 word individually". The verse-final chanted word of Psalms 72:15 is a lone atom, and the verse, as MAM-simple has it, is וִיחִ֗י וְיִתֶּן־לוֹ֮ מִזְּהַ֢ב שְׁ֫בָ֥א וְיִתְפַּלֵּ֣ל בַּעֲד֣וֹ תָמִ֑יד כׇּל־הַ֝יּ֗וֹם יְבָרְכֶֽנְהוּ׃

## 1. Summary: MAM and the Aleppo Codex have one U+05BD; the Leningrad Codex has a second, after the silluq

1. **MAM has one U+05BD, on the kaf, and so has the Aleppo Codex: established, the Codex from Ben's reading of it on 2026-09-10.** Ben read the word in mgketer.org's image of Psalm 72, which is on the Codex's leaf 253v: it has one stroke, under the kaf, which slants like a merkha but cannot plausibly be read as one. His crop is `gh-pages/img/aleppo-253v-Ps72v15-yevarkhenhu.png`; its source notes are in `doc/meteg-after-silluq-snips/README.md`. mgketer.org's page for Psalms 72 is `https://www.mgketer.org/mikra/27/72/1/mg/106`. MAM's source (`in/mam-ws/D1-Psalms.json` line 2148) has יְבָרְﬞכֶֽנְהוּ in a `נוסח` template whose note gives the Aleppo Codex's form as יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהוּ, with a ḥataf under the resh where MAM has a sheva with a varika. MAM's introduction quotes the Aleppo Codex with the same form in its list of ḥatafs under non-guttural letters (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch5.mediawiki` line 737, under the heading at line 716). Both quotations are about the ḥataf, so neither was made to record the meteg, and both agree with MAM's text. MAM has the meteg as the Aleppo Codex has it wherever that manuscript survives (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch3.mediawiki` line 1328), and this verse is on the Aleppo Codex's leaf 253v, which has Psalms 71:18–73:10 (MAM's index of the Aleppo Codex, `in/mam-ws-intro/index-aleppo.mediawiki` line 613).
2. **The Leningrad Codex has a stroke under the kaf and one under the he, as UXLC 3.9, WLC 4.22 and WLC 4.20 record: Ben's reading of the manuscript, 2026-09-10.** UXLC 3.9 records יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהֽוּ׃ with no note on the word (`in/UXLC-39/Psalms.xml` line 13314), and the two WLC conversions have the same codepoints (line 33165 of `out/wlc422-u/1verses_08_2cps.json`, line 33140 of `out/wlc420-u/1verses_08_2cps.json`). Ben read the word in an image of the Leningrad Codex's folio 380A (`uxlc/data/lci_augrecs.json`, the record at line 15070). Sefaria's image of the folio is `https://manuscripts.sefaria.org/leningrad-color/BIB_LENCDX_F380A.jpg`, and tanach.us's page for the verse is `https://tanach.us/Tanach.xml?Ps72:15`. The stroke under the he stands farther left and a little lower than he expected, but plainly belongs to this line and not to the line below. The word is in the early middle of line 3, where the estimator (`page_and_guesses` in `py/py_uxlc/my_uxlc_location.py`) said column 2, line 5.5. His crop is `gh-pages/img/leningrad-380A-col2-line3-Ps72v15-yevarkhenhu.png`; its source notes are in `doc/meteg-after-silluq-snips/README.md`.
3. **mgketer's transcription of Mikra'ot Gedolot ha-Keter records one U+05BD, on the kaf: established.** Its form is יְבָרֲכֶֽנְהוּ (`C:/Users/BenDe/GitRepos/MAM-private/mgketer/out/D1-Psalms/mgketer-json-massaged/D1-Psalms_72.json` line 646).
4. **Established from MAM's form and Ben's 2026-09-10 reading of the Leningrad Codex: MAM's stress is penultimate, so the Leningrad Codex's U+05BD on the he is after the silluq.** MAM's one U+05BD, on the kaf, is its silluq, and Phonetic MAM's `jta` is `y^.va.r^.!khen.hu`. Koren's stress is penultimate too (item 5), the Simanim Tanakh has its two marks where Koren has its (item 6), and the parallels in section 4 of `doc/meteg-after-silluq-job-4-12.md` have the same suffix, stressed on the syllable before it. The syllables are those of MAM's 1 Kings 7:37, לְכֻלָּֽהְנָֽה׃: a stressed closed syllable, then an open last syllable with a long vowel, which has the second U+05BD.
5. **Koren has a U+05BD on the bet and on the kaf, none on the he, and a silent simple sheva under the nun: Ben's readings, 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-23.** Ben's judgment on 2026-09-10 is that the mark on the bet is highly unlikely to be a silluq, so in Koren the word has a meteg and then the silluq, and its stress is penultimate. Koren thus agrees with MAM on the stress, has a meteg on the bet that MAM lacks, and has nothing on the he, where UXLC and WLC record a U+05BD. Ben reported on 2026-09-23 that the simple sheva under the nun is silent.
6. **The Simanim Tanakh has a U+05BD on the bet and on the kaf, none on the he, a vocal simple sheva under the resh, and a silent simple sheva under the nun: Ben's readings, 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-23.** Its two marks are where Koren has its. Its vocal sheva under the resh stands where the Aleppo Codex as MAM quotes it, UXLC 3.9, WLC and mgketer have a ḥataf pataḥ, and Ben takes it to follow from the edition's editorial policy rather than from a choice made for this word; `doc/simanim-tanakh-signs.md` records no policy on ḥatafs under non-guttural letters. MAM's policy does the same thing: its introduction says that a ḥataf under a non-guttural letter in the Aleppo Codex or the Leningrad Codex marks a vocal sheva, and that MAM has in its place a special sign over the letter with the sheva (`in/mam-ws-intro/ch5.mediawiki` line 718). That sign is the varika of MAM's form here. The vocality judgments concern two different shevas: the resh's sheva is vocal, while Ben reported on 2026-09-23 that the nun's sheva is silent.
7. **Established from the two named artifacts: the earlier screen lists Psalms 72:15, and the post-stress-meteg survey now treats Psalms 72:15.** `doc/meteg-after-silluq-screen-against-uxlc-and-wlc.md` puts Psalms 72:15 in its class (v) against both UXLC 3.9 and WLC 4.22 (its section 4). The survey's post-silluq page, `gh-pages/post-stress-meteg-post-silluq.html`, now includes Psalms 72:15 in its case register and links to the manuscript-image page for the verse.
8. **Not found in the sources checked: neither Yeivin's *Introduction to the Tiberian Masorah* nor Breuer's *The Cantillation of Scripture* discusses whether Psalms 72:15 has a meteg after the silluq; Ben's Da'at Miqra observation is a distinct positive source.** Of those first two books, only *The Cantillation of Scripture* cites Psalms 72:15, once, as an unpointed verse-division example (ch. 10 §4, `C10-S001.md` line 365 under `C:/Users/BenDe/GitRepos/MAM-private/masorah-books/books/cos/md-export-of-docx/`). The ga'aya that the U+05BD on the he would be, Breuer's type j after a mafsik, is named for 1 Kings 7:37 alone (*The Cantillation of Scripture*, ch. 8 §46, footnote 81). Separately, Ben reported on 2026-09-23 that in *Da'at Miqra* Breuer notes the Leningrad Codex's meteg after the silluq at Psalms 72:15; the source page itself has not been independently inspected for this report.
9. **Cambridge Add. 1753 and Codex Sassoon 1053 have the silluq alone: Ben's readings, 2026-09-23.** Neither manuscript has the later meteg recorded in the Leningrad Codex. Ben supplied and interpreted both crops; Codex did not independently inspect the pointed Hebrew. The Cambridge Add. 1753 crop's source site and manuscript page or folio are not recorded. Ben obtained the Codex Sassoon 1053 crop from Masoretica, whose source link is recorded in `doc/meteg-after-silluq-snips/README.md`.

## 4. How the original figures were made

The scripts below were throwaway scripts and are not tracked.

1. One spelled out this atom codepoint by codepoint in every source available to the original run and in Phonetic MAM.
2. One wrote the original file from a template. It lifted every pointed form and the `jta` from the data; asserted that the note's and the introduction's quotations of the Aleppo Codex agree, that UXLC 3.9 and both WLC conversions have the same codepoints, and how many U+05BD each form has; and checked the result with `has_std_mark_order`.
3. One estimated the Leningrad Codex's column and line for the verse-final atom and built the Sefaria, tanach.us and mgketer.org links in section 1. The tracked `py/main_verse_links.py` builds links of the same kinds.
