Regions of ink

Regions drafted by an AI model, and only partly reviewed by a human so far. Claude, an AI model, placed the seeds, cuts, overlaps and strips of supplied vellum on this page, made the groups, and judged every residual piece of level 1; the regions come from the seeds by a watershed on the photograph's luminance. Ben has begun to review them: each place changed from his answers has a note that says so, shown when the pointer rests on it in the layer of cuts or in the tables. Read the rest as a draft.

Each crop of the Leningrad Codex is drawn with its region-SVG: its ink as regions, one or more for each sign, each in one group, and overlaps, ink that two signs are proposed to share, each in two groups or more. The checkboxes beside each image switch that image's layers, and its menu sets its size. The profile, and what each layer means, are in doc/ink-region-representation.md; this page is generated by py/main_pen_stroke.py regions-render.

Orange: a supplied mark. The crop shows little or no ink of it; it is there because the transcription or the layout implies it. Teal: restored ink, proposed where the image shows the ink has flaked. Tan: supplied vellum, a strip of the page between two signs that the photograph's blur joins, taken out of the ink. None of the three is ever drawn in black or in a group's or a region's colour, and no other layer uses any of them.

Slate: ink of another word or line, which the crop holds but is not its subject, in every layer; in the ink by meaning it is a faint tint of slate.
