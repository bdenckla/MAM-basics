# Evaluation of the English translation of the Introduction to MAM, 2026-11-22

State: completed 2026-11-22; not yet acted on

Written by Claude (Opus 5.5) on 2026-11-22, as Phase 2 of
[PLAN-english-intro.md](PLAN-english-intro.md), whose section "Origin, and whose words are
whose" quotes Ben's request. His item 2 asked that the session "evaluate that translation and
suggest tweaks where it seems to fall short". Seven sub-agents evaluated one slice each, in
parallel. Before assembling their reports here, the session that started them checked at least
one substantive finding of each slice against the files, and checked by script that every Hebrew
run in every report occurs in the mirrors or in the English pages, so that none was typed. One
finding failed its check and is corrected in place, marked as the assembler's correction:
`appendices-8`. Everything else below is the evaluators' text, and no human has reviewed it.
Nothing has been changed on English Wikisource or Hebrew Wikisource.

## What was compared

- **The English:** the article views of the seven pages under `User:Dovi/Miqra according to
  the Masorah/Introduction` on English Wikisource, read on 2026-11-22, at the revisions that
  each slice names. The plan's section "The English on English Wikisource" lists them. The
  English is under CC BY-SA, as its translator's note says, and the findings quote it only in
  short phrases.
- **The Hebrew:** the mirror `in/mam-ws-intro/`, at the revisions that its `manifest.json`
  records, the latest of them made on 2026-11-07. Each finding cites it by file and line.
- **Not compared:** Chapters 1, 4 and 5, which have no translation; the edition's main English
  page, which is outside the seven; and any manuscript. A finding about a manuscript reports
  what MAM's introduction says of it.

Most of the English was last edited in January 2025, and the Hebrew has been revised since. So
many differences are the Hebrew having moved on, rather than errors of translation; those
findings have the category drift.

## How to read a finding

Each finding has an id, `<slice>-<n>`, and a category: mistranslation, omission, addition,
terminology, drift, english or markup. It is substantive where a reader would be misinformed,
and minor otherwise. It gives the English location as a section, a line of the slice's
plain-text extraction of the article view, and a short quoted phrase; the Hebrew location as a
file and line of the mirror; and a suggested tweak, replacement English ready to paste. The
plain-text extractions are not kept in this repository, so the quoted phrase and its section are
the lasting locator. Each slice also has a section map, which pairs its English sections with
their Hebrew counterparts and says how fully each is covered.

## Five problems that run through the pages

1. **Drift.** The data sheet and its list of corrections were frozen on 12 September 2026, but
   the English root, the Abstract, the Appendices and the Technical Guide still present them as
   live (`root-abstract-5`, `root-abstract-23`, `appendices-1`, `data-sheet-guide-1`). The
   translator's note gives MAM's license as CC BY-SA 3.0, where the Hebrew now links 4.0
   (`root-abstract-1`). The Hebrew has relabeled and expanded its roster of sigla, so English
   sigla now name the wrong manuscripts (`appendices-10`, `appendices-11`, `appendices-15`).
   Chapter 3 now has sections that the English lacks, such as "Foundations of the Text"
   (`ch3a-5`) and the Aleppo Codex's departures from the Masorah (`ch3a-11`).
2. **Wrong references and names.** Chapter 3's book lists convert Hebrew numerals wrongly in 17
   items, eleven of them giving Deuteronomy chapters 40 to 44 (`ch3a-17`, `ch3a-18`), and the
   chapter's later lists and table have more wrong verse numbers (`ch3b-26`, `ch3c-11`,
   `ch3c-12`). The English names Moses Isserles five times where the Hebrew names the Ramah,
   Rabbi Meir Abulafia (`ch3c-32`), and the Leningrad Codex where its quotation names the Keter,
   the Aleppo Codex (`ch3b-19`).
3. **Terms of art read as ordinary words.** The Minḥat Shai's "with a shewa" becomes "in vain"
   (`ch1-ch2-10`, `ch1-ch2-11`). Hiddur mitzvah becomes strict fulfillment (`ch1-ch2-26`), the
   accent names oleh-weyored and atnaḥ are translated as words (`ch1-ch2-19`), and the Hebrew's
   communities and testimonies become "witnesses" (`ch3a-29`, `ch3b-6`).
4. **An unfinished draft.** The translator's own query marks remain, 44 of them in `ch3c`'s
   lines alone. Some passages are left in Hebrew (`ch3c-35`), and some sentences are garbled
   (`ch3c-36`, `ch3c-42`).
5. **Terminology off the house rules.** Transliteration is inconsistent, with Minḥat Shai beside
   Minchat Shai, and with a backtick for ayin. The English also has "upper" and "lower" for the
   Decalogue's two cantillations, whose names the house rules keep in Hebrew letters
   (`data-sheet-guide-15`).

## What to fix first

1. The factual errors of item 2 above, and the misattached sigla and the two manuscripts said
   to be unused in the Appendices (`appendices-10` to `appendices-13`, and `appendices-15`).
2. The expired statements of status in item 1: the data sheet, the list of corrections and
   MAM's license. Each is a quick edit.
3. The terms of art in item 3.
4. A retranslation of the drifted passages from the current Hebrew, starting with Chapter 3's
   "Foundations of the Text" and the Aleppo Codex's departures from the Masorah.
5. The terminology and the typos, in one pass.

Phase 4 of the plan applies the tweaks that Ben accepts, on his word, and records each
finding's disposition in this record's single update file,
`intro-en-evaluation-2026-11-22-update.md`.

### Findings

1. **root-abstract-1. License version out of date** (drift, substantive). English: Introduction, To the Reader, line 14, "a Creative Commons license (CC BY-SA 3.0)". Hebrew: summary.mediawiki:20. The Hebrew summary links MAM's license to the CC BY-SA 4.0 deed, as the English Abstract's link (line 29) also does, so the note's version is out of date. **Suggested tweak:** change the version to "CC BY-SA 4.0".

2. **root-abstract-2. Contents list numbers non-chapters as items 6 to 8** (markup, minor). English: Introduction, Contents, lines 27–41 (and Abstract lines 71–85, by transclusion), "Partial English Translation". Hebrew: root.mediawiki:9–18. The list is numbered, so the Appendices, the spreadsheet and its guide render as items 6, 7 and 8 after Chapters 1–5, as if they were chapters; the Hebrew root uses bullets. **Suggested tweak:** make the list a bulleted list, starting each item with `*` instead of `#`.

3. **root-abstract-3. Untranslated chapters lead to an empty page or to nothing** (markup, minor). English: Introduction, Contents, lines 27, 33 and 35, "(not yet translated)". Hebrew: root.mediawiki:9, 12, 13. The Chapter 1 link opens an English page that has only a heading and an empty footnotes section, and Chapters 4 and 5 have no link at all, while the Hebrew root links all three. **Suggested tweak:** for Chapter 1, `(not yet translated; [[:he:ויקיטקסט:מבוא למקרא על פי המסורה/פרק א|Hebrew original]])`; for Chapter 4, `(not yet translated; [[:he:ויקיטקסט:מבוא למקרא על פי המסורה/פרק ד|Hebrew original]])`; for Chapter 5, `(not yet translated; [[:he:ויקיטקסט:מבוא למקרא על פי המסורה/פרק ה|Hebrew original]])`.

4. **root-abstract-4. Misspelled subsection title** (english, minor). English: Introduction, Contents, line 29, "Ḥatafim in Non-Gutteral Letters". Hebrew: root.mediawiki:10; ch2.mediawiki:260. "Gutteral" should be "Guttural", as the English Chapter 2 page spells its heading, and the sentence around the title is awkward. **Suggested tweak:** "Chapter 2: Styling Miqra for the Reader (so far only part of the subsection "Ḥatafim in Non-Guttural Letters" has a draft translation)". If the Chapter 2 page renames that heading, keep this title in step.

5. **root-abstract-5. Spreadsheet and guide presented as live** (drift, substantive). English: Introduction, Contents, lines 39–41 (and Abstract lines 83–85, by transclusion), "The Technical Basis for the Project Organized in a Spreadsheet". Hebrew: root.mediawiki:16–18. The Hebrew now lists both under an archive label, says that the spreadsheet, its changes tab included, was frozen on 12 September 2026 and is no longer updated, and that Hebrew Wikisource is the maintained source, and calls the guides historical; the English still offers the spreadsheet as the project's technical basis. **Suggested tweak:** replace the two items with a separate group: "**Data spreadsheet (archive):** the project's historical data spreadsheet, including its changes tab, was frozen on 12 September 2026 and is no longer updated; Hebrew Wikisource is the maintained source. **Historical guides to the spreadsheet:** English | Hebrew". The navigation bar's two spreadsheet links, outside this evaluation, need the same change.

Hebrew: summary.mediawiki:7, also 13, 28 and 42. The Hebrew calls MAM an edition for reading and study throughout and titles its section 2 "Tiqqun Qor'im and study edition", but the English never mentions study; the relative clause that ends the English sentence also attaches to the manuscripts rather than to the edition. **Suggested tweak:** keeping the existing links, "It is an edition for reading and study, based on the Aleppo Codex and the manuscripts close to it. 11. **root-abstract-11. Mechon Mamre's lack of documentation is stated twice** (english, minor). English: Abstract, 1. Free Content License, line 33, "But it lacks documentation, and". Hebrew: summary.mediawiki:23. The item says that the edition is undocumented and says so again two sentences later; it also joins asking permission and paying with "and" where the Hebrew has "or", and leaves out the Hebrew's limit to short passages and its ban on adding any correction. **Suggested tweak:** from that phrase to the end of the item: "But it is not available under a free license: you may make limited personal use of it (short passages) without asking permission or paying, but you may not copy it or improve it (for example, by posting it on your website together with a commentary, a translation, or any correction or added feature). 13. **root-abstract-13. The stress-helper sentence hides where the second accent goes and why** (mistranslation, minor). English: Abstract, 2. The Hebrew says that in every word whose accent is written elsewhere, a second copy of the accent was added on the stressed syllable, so that the reader can tell whether the word is stressed on its last syllable or earlier; the English names neither the syllable nor the purpose. **Suggested tweak:** for that sentence, "For instance, in every word whose accent is written on a syllable other than the stressed one (pashta, zarqa, segol and telisha), a second copy of the accent has been added on the stressed syllable, so that the reader can tell whether the word is stressed on its last syllable (milra) or earlier (mil'el)."

### Overall

Section 9.1 is a close and mostly accurate paragraph-by-paragraph translation, but section 4.6 is a single sentence with two footnotes, and its first footnote takes the Minḥat Shai's בשוא, “with a shewa”, for “in vain”, which reverses the quotation's point (ch1-ch2-10, ch1-ch2-11). The other substantive errors are the accent names oleh-weyored and atnaḥ rendered as ordinary words (ch1-ch2-19), hiddur mitzvah rendered as strict fulfillment (ch1-ch2-26), a ḥataf ḥiriq sentence that makes the shewa the Aleppo Codex's peculiarity (ch1-ch2-14), and a section on ḥataf vowels that never says what MAM has (ch1-ch2-9). ### Overall

The slice is a close, generally faithful translation of an older Hebrew text, and the bracketed Hebrew words in its lists match the mirror point for point, but its prose has many small slips and it now trails the Hebrew in substance. Its most systematic defect is the chapter-and-verse references, converted wrongly from Hebrew numerals in 17 of the 126 items of the book lists, eleven of them Deuteronomy 30-34 written as nonexistent chapters 40-44. The Hebrew has since gained a section on the seven-manuscript basis of the Torah's text, a section on reading the textual notes, the 2 or 3 places where the Aleppo Codex departs from the Masorah, the nine differences between the scrolls of the communities, and new data (Exodus 36:21, Numbers 33:52's annotation, new totals), so the English now misstates MAM's manuscript basis and the standing of Breuer's and Ofer's claims about the Yemenite scrolls. Terminology is uneven: Breuer's מסירות is left in Hebrew letters or called traditions, evidence becomes "witness", the communities' scrolls become "scrolls of all witnesses", and the scribes' tradition has four English names on the page. An editor should first correct the 17 references and the substantive mistranslations (the Yeivin quotation, "scrolls of all witnesses", the citations of footnotes 2, 4 and 7), then translate the drifted passages, starting with the Aleppo Codex's deviations and the "Foundations of the Text" section.

### Overall

The slice renders the lists of conjoined words and of large, small, dotted and inverted letters faithfully on the whole, and its Hebrew forms match the mirror's except at Deuteronomy 32:6, Numbers 27:5 and Leviticus 8:23.
