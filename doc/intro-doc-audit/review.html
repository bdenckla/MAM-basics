One written word read as two different words: three cases or four?

Chapter 2 of the MAM Introduction counts three cases; the Introduction’s template appendix counts four and includes 2 Samuel 8:3.

Ketiv is the written form; qere is what is read aloud. This category is a single written word assigned two spoken words whose letters differ from the written form, rather than merely splitting that form at a space. The appendix lists 2 Samuel 8:3, Ezekiel 9:11, 2 Kings 18:27 and Isaiah 36:12.

In three verses in Scripture there is a qere note assigning two qere words to one ketiv word.

Aleppo Codex qere: “בנהר פרת” (“at the River Euphrates”), both words; however, the maqaf for “בנהר” is missing, as are the space and pointing for “פְּרָֽת”, which is not written.

The other manuscript traditions treat the final word as a separate “read but not written” item, so a count of traditional Masorah notes could depend on the intended convention.

Decision: Decide whether chapter 2 is meant to count the four cases used by MAM’s template appendix. If so, it needs 2 Samuel 8:3 and a total of four. If it is counting a narrower manuscript convention, state that convention and explain why Samuel is excluded.
