## Concrete uncertainty and scope decisions

Four possible manuscript-reading omissions remain: Deuteronomy 3:22, Jeremiah
48:32, Daniel 12:2 and Nehemiah 6:19. Two Aleppo ketiv forms at 2 Samuel 24:16
and 24:18 have unresolved vowel function. Jeremiah 48:20 and 50:6 are already
listed qere positions with ketiv-only note evidence; their attribution gaps are
retained. 2 Samuel 6:5 has the body-only form וּֽבְצֶלְצֱלִֽים and no doc-note.
These are nine concrete reading/function/source-object cases, not confirmed
omissions. The reader review shows all nine, including the existing Deuteronomy
and Jeremiah cases.

Psalm 17:14’s list entry explicitly names Sassoon1053 (`ש1`); the heading names
Aleppo or Leningrad. The note has other manuscripts and reconstructed Aleppo
practice, while Leningrad expressly has no ḥataf. That framing decision is shown
separately. The prior Ezekiel 17:17 attribution question and anonymous specimens
remain in the existing semantic list exception records; lexical matching has not
silently turned them into attributed manuscript claims.

## Body functions and concrete controls

The selected edition text has 536 non-guttural ḥataf occurrences: 305 divine-name
or prefix/function cases, 224 contextual short-o forms, six Aramaic reduced-segol
forms and one unresolved 2 Samuel 6:5 form. All 63 distinct bare-letter form
groups were checked independently. The morphological labels are contextual
inferences unless the doc-note expressly identifies the function.

Concrete short-o/ḥolam controls include רֳאִ֑י and רֹאִֽי in Genesis 16:13;
תִקֳּבֶ֑נּוּ in Numbers 23:25 and לָקֹ֤ב in Numbers 23:11 / 24:10;
יֶהְדֳּפֶ֑נּוּ in Numbers 35:20 and לַהֲדֹ֥ף in Deuteronomy 6:19;
יִגֳּפֶ֑נּוּ in 1 Samuel 26:10 and יִגֹּ֧ף in Exodus 21:35; שִׁבֳּלִים and
שִׁבֹּ֜לֶת at Judges 12:6; and Ruth’s לַקֳּטָה forms with לִלְקֹט֙ at Ruth 2:8.
These comparisons provide evidence beyond the fact that a vowel is retained.

Daniel 2:30’s note expressly calls the gimel vowel in גֱּלִ֣י morphological segol.
Ezra 8:25’s note expressly calls the qoph vowel in וָאֶשְׁקֳלָ֣ה the morphological
ḥolam preserved in ketiv. Introduction chapter 2 line 161 explains the divine-name
qere function; chapter 5 line 615 and Psalm 144:15’s note preserve both
שֱׁיְהֹוָ֥ה (Aleppo) and שֶׁיֲהֹוָ֥ה (Leningrad).

[Morphology controls](all-books-hataf-morphology-controls.json) retain the checker’s
actual form/verse matches. [Assessments](all-books-hataf-assessments.json) retain
all 63 groups and all occurrence locations. [Independent check](all-books-hataf-independent-check.json)
retains the separate 43-note triage, including the Psalm 65:10 inline-template
artifact subsequently resolved as already listed. The independent reviewer’s
initial Jeremiah omission interpretation was corrected before any definite finding
was published: the list already has those qere positions.
