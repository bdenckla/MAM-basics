# Sources the pen-stroke work has cited

State: live; the books, articles and PDFs cited up to 2026-11-01

This lists the books, articles and PDFs that the pen-stroke work's research cited, so that they
outlive the sessions that found them. Ben asked for it on 2026-10-31, as the commander session
"Commander for Claude's forests" relayed his words: "In a recently-archived session regarding
pen-stroke work, I feel like there were some references to some books and at least one PDF. I'd
like those to be recorded somewhere git-tracked". The representation itself is in
[pen-stroke-representation.md](pen-stroke-representation.md).

## Who cited them, and what the statuses mean

Every judgment below is the named agent's, not Ben's, and none was checked again for this list.

- **The planner:** the session "Investigate a pen-stroke manuscript representation", 2026-10-30,
  and its two web-research sub-agents. The first surveyed existing work at the level of strokes;
  the second surveyed encoding standards, from which only its books, articles and PDFs are taken
  here.
- **The ductus research:** a Claude sub-agent's web research of 2026-10-31 on how square-script
  letters are stroked and what their heads are called, for the session that took Ben's round-2
  feedback. That session checked two of its claims at their sources, as noted below.
- **The commander:** the session above, which read one PDF on 2026-10-31.
- **Round 3:** the session that traced two whole lines, 2026-10-31.
- **Round 4:** the session that re-traced those lines' bases, 2026-10-31.
- **Round 5:** the session that swept those lines' letters with each crop's one nib,
  2026-11-01.

A **read** source was read by the agent named. **Snippet only** means the agent knew it only
from a search summary, a title or a catalogue listing; what such a summary said is reported as
the summary's, not as fact. **Not read** means the agent cited it without reading it, often
because it is a PDF or a book not online. **Not marked** means the agent's report gives no
status.

Ben, 2026-10-31, of the books by Yardeni, Beit-Arié and Sirat that could not be read online: "I am
open to ordering these, scanning, and doing OCR on them."

## Hebrew script and its palaeography

1. **Ada Yardeni, *The Book of Hebrew Script*** (Hebrew 1991; English, Carta 1997, British Library
   2002, 3rd ed. Carta 2014). Carta's listing:
   <https://store.carta-jerusalem.com/hebrew-script/78-the-book-of-hebrew-script-9789652208118.html>;
   the Internet Archive's copy, lending-restricted:
   <https://archive.org/details/bookofhebrewscri0000yard>. Bears on how letters are stroked and
   what their parts are called. Not read: neither the planner nor the ductus research could read
   it online. A retailer's contents listing showed the planner's first sub-agent a chapter on hand
   movements and stroke direction, which it marked unverified; whether the book draws heads as
   strokes of their own is the ductus research's unfinished question. **Read in part by round
   4:** pages 216 to 218 and 293 to 297, in copies Ben supplied and keeps privately. Decision 18
   of the schema note follows its figure 247, an exercise in drawing the parts of the letters in
   the 10th-century Eastern book-hand.
2. **Ada Yardeni, "Palaeography as a Tool"**, PDF:
   <https://www.adayardeni.com/paleo/Palaeography-as-a-Tool.pdf>. Bears on each letter's core
   strokes. Not read, by either agent; a search summary told both that it discusses stroke
   direction and each letter's few basic strokes.
3. **Stav Axenfeld, interview with Ada Yardeni**, AlefAlefAlef, about 2018:
   <https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/ada-yardeni-interview/>. Read by the ductus research: she studied
   the direction of the letters' strokes, which she said varies with the implement, and coined
   her names for letter parts in the 1991 book.
4. **Malachi Beit-Arié, Edna Engel and Ada Yardeni, *Specimens of Mediaeval Hebrew Scripts*,
   vol. I, *Oriental and Yemenite Scripts*** (Israel Academy, 1987/88). Listing:
   <https://isdistribution.com/?p=1380787>; Perani's review:
   <https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/627032>. Bears on letter charts of 10th- and 11th-century
   Oriental hands, which the planner's first sub-agent judged "a target for tracing, not a
   dataset". Snippet only: the book is printed, and both agents knew its letter charts from
   listings.
5. **Malachi Beit-Arié, *Hebrew Codicology***, Hebrew edition, ed. Zofia Lasman (Jerusalem and
   Hamburg, 2021), CC BY 4.0: <https://www.fdr.uni-hamburg.de/record/9697>. Bears on the pen and the
   number of strokes a script needs. Read in part by the planner, which reported that it calls the
   pen קולמוס throughout, never tells a reed from a quill in any passage found, refers writing
   implements to Löw's *Graphische Requisiten* and to Krauss in a footnote, and says, in the
   planner's English, that the script type "determines the number of pen strokes needed to
   produce its letters". Downloaded with Ben's
   approval on 2026-10-30; a copy and its extracted text are kept, untracked, in the pen-stroke
   working folder of the MAM-basics clone that holds the work. The planner's first sub-agent cited
   *Hebrew Codicology* as record 9349, <https://www.fdr.uni-hamburg.de/record/9349>, DOI
   10.25592/uhhfdm.9349, a PDF it did not read; the planner named the English edition of 2022,
   18.3 MB, which was not downloaded. Which edition record 9349 is was not checked.
6. **Colette Sirat, *Hebrew Manuscripts of the Middle Ages*** (Cambridge, 2002), **and *Writing as
   Handwork*** (Brepols, 2006). Bear on cutting the pen and on writing's movements. Snippet only:
   a search summary told the ductus research of a figure on sharpening a quill (p. 113) in the
   first and of a part on postures and movements in the second.
7. **Solomon A. Birnbaum, "Alphabet, Hebrew"**, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*, 2nd ed. (2007), vol. 1,
   pp. 689–728:
   <https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/alphabet-hebrew>.
   Read by the ductus research: thick horizontals and thin downstrokes, letters' heads by about the
   7th century, and the reed and the quill; no stroke order and no account of how a head is made.
8. **Edna Engel, "Style of Hebrew script in the 10th–11th centuries in light of dated Geniza
   documents"**, *Te'uda* 15 (1998/99), in Hebrew, PDF: <https://core.ac.uk/download/223693965.pdf>.
   Bears on the subtypes of the Oriental square script of the codices' time. Not marked.
9. **Glatzer, "The Aleppo Codex: codicological and paleographical aspects"**, *Sefunot* 19
   (1989), 167–276; record:
   <https://mbh.huma-num.fr/en/aleppo-codex-codicological-and-paleographical-aspects>. **Ofer**
   on Cassuto's notes on the Aleppo Codex, *Sefunot* 19, 277–344, and *Textus* 26 (2016), 173–198;
   and Ofer, *The Masora on Scripture and Its Methods* (De Gruyter, 2019), chapter 8, DOI
   10.1515/9783110594560. Bear on the Aleppo Codex's hand and its writers. Not marked; cited by the
   planner's first sub-agent.
10. **Phillips, on the Leningrad Codex's colophon**, *Tyndale Bulletin*, 2017, PDF:
    <https://tyndalebulletin.org/article/29421.pdf>. Bears on whether one man wrote the letters and
    the marks; the sub-agent reported that the colophon on page F474A says Samuel ben Jacob wrote,
    pointed and supplied the Masorah, which Phillips calls unusual. Not read (a PDF).
11. **Vincent Beiler, on Evr. II B 17 and II B 1150+**: abstracts,
    <https://cambridge.academia.edu/VincentBeiler>, and a Cambridge University Library Q&A,
    <https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/node/1350321>. Bears on a scribe and a vocalizer working apart in
    one workshop. Snippet only: the sub-agent knew the argument from abstracts.
12. **Khan, *The Tiberian Pronunciation Tradition*** (Open Book Publishers, 2020), vol. I: <https://www.openbookcollective.org/books/book/4204>. Bears on the
    writing of the vowels and accents. Not read (a PDF).
13. **Nehring, Gordon and Rabin, on telling seemingly identical inks apart by scanning µXRF**,
    *Journal of Cultural Heritage* 57 (2022), 142–148; record:
    <https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/distinguishing-between-seemingly-identical-inks-using-scanning-%C2%B5x/>.
    Bears on telling original, corrected and re-inked strokes apart. Not marked.
14. **Sharva Gogawale and others, "Classifying Medieval Manuscripts by Pen and Support"**,
    *Anthology of Computers and the Humanities* 3, 2025-11-21:
    <https://anthology.ach.org/volumes/vol0003/classifying-medieval-manuscripts-by-pen-support/>.
    Bears on the reed against the quill. Read by the ductus research, the abstract only: a network
    tells quill from reed in medieval Hebrew manuscripts from stroke morphology.

## Scribes' guides

15. **Jen Taylor Friedman, Beit Yosef practice sheet** (2008), PDF:
    <https://www.hasoferet.com/images/beityosef.pdf>. Bears on how letters are built from pen
    strokes. Snippet only for the ductus research. **Read by the commander** on 2026-10-31, whose
    reading confirmed the claims the ductus research had from search summaries: the sheet builds
    letters from rectangles, verticals and parallelograms, the pen's angle is the key to the
    parallelograms, and the pen is pulled, never pushed. It corrected one: the fine details, such
    as beit's corners, tag and heel, are pulled out of the wet ink with the same nib's point
    tipped sideways, not with a smaller pen. A copy is kept, untracked, beside the ductus research
    in the same working folder. **Read again by round 5** on 2026-11-01, for what it says about
    the pen. Beyond what the commander found, it offers turning the nib during a stroke, to make a
    wedge, as one way to make an asymmetric leg, and makes a tilted vav with the nib at a steep
    angle to the line; its ayin's foot is two strokes or more, begun at the bottom and pulled
    upward. It teaches modern Torah-scroll (STaM) practice in the Beit Yosef script, crowns
    included, so what it says is that practice's, not the Leningrad Codex's scribe's.
16. **Mishnat Soferim**, the Mishnah Berurah's rules for the letters, translated by Jen Taylor
    Friedman (2006): <https://www.hasoferet.com/halakha-for-scribes/mishnat-soferim/>. Bears on the
    letters' parts and heads. Read by the ductus research; the round-2 feedback session read again
    its words on the ayin, whose first mark is like a yod, its face ideally tilted a little upward.
17. **Keset HaSofer, chapter 5**, in English:
    <https://hasoferet.com/halakha-for-scribes/keset-ha-sofer/chapter-5>. Bears on the letters'
    parts. Not marked; cited by the planner's first sub-agent.
18. **Mordechai Pinchas (Marc Michaels), "Alefbet"**: <https://www.sofer.co.uk/alefbet>. Bears on
    the letters' parts. Read by the ductus research.
19. **Jen Taylor Friedman, "Quills, part 2"** (2009-10-20):
    <https://hatam-soferet.dreamwidth.org/471870.html>, **and "Quills, part 6"** (2009-10-27):
    <https://hatam-soferet.dreamwidth.org/475148.html>. Bear on the reed against the quill. Read by
    the ductus research.
20. **Richard Wideman, "Learn Hebrew calligraphy"**, Calligrascape:
    <https://calligrascape.com/learn-hebrew-calligraphy/>. Bears on basic strokes. Read by the
    ductus research, whose tool could not see its images.
21. **Mark Farnadi-Jerusálmi's EPHE thesis**, defended 2021-03-11:
    <https://www.ephe.psl.eu/avis-de-soutenance-doctorat-mark-farnadi-jerusalmi>. Bears on Beit Yosef
    and Arizal letter parts against four medieval Erfurt scrolls. Snippet only: known from the
    defence's announcement.

## Hebrew type design

22. **Shani Avni, "Hebrew type anatomy"**, TypeTogether, September 2021:
    <https://www.typetogether.com/hebrew-type-anatomy>. Bears on what a letter's thick start is: an
    "instroke". Read by the ductus research; the round-2 feedback session read again its account
    of the instroke as at first a blunt reed's stroke moving inward from left to right, which
    leaves a triangle where it begins, without saying whether it is a stroke of its own.
23. **Avraham Cornfeld, "Anatomy of Hebrew Type"**, AlefAlefAlef:
    <https://alefalefalef.co.il/en/anatomy-of-type/>. Read by the ductus research.
24. **Meir Sadan, "What is a 'serif' in Hebrew?"**, Medium, 2017-10-11:
    <https://medium.com/@meirsadan/what-is-a-serif-in-hebrew-8014f1f63f63>, **and "An Introduction
    to Hebrew Type"**, Medium. Bear on the names of letter heads and on the pen's angle. Snippet
    only: the site refused the ductus research.
25. **Adi Stern, MA dissertation on Hebrew type** (2003):
    <https://issuu.com/gerryleonidas/docs/2003_dissertation_adistern>. Snippet only: the page
    returned 404 to the ductus research.
26. **Monotype, "Typography terms"**: <https://monotype.com/studio/typography-terms>. Bears on
    "finial". Read by the ductus research.

## Computational work

27. **Sober and Levin, "Computer aided restoration of handwritten character
    strokes"**, *Computer-Aided Design* 89 (2017), 12–24: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07038v1>; used
    in Faigenbaum-Golovin and others, *PNAS* 2016, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1522200113. Bears on a stroke as
    a spline swept by a pen of varying radius, which the planner's first sub-agent judged "the
    closest published formalism to the project's target representation". Not marked.
28. **Popović, Dhali and Schomaker**, on the scribes of the Great Isaiah
    Scroll, *PLOS ONE* 2021, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0249769, and the Enoch study, *PLOS ONE* 2025,
    DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0323185. Bear on statistics of letter shape from binarized ink. Not
    marked by the planner's first sub-agent; a search hit, not read, for the ductus research.
29. **The Groningen writer-identification features:** Bulacu and Schomaker, *IEEE TPAMI* 29(4)
    (2007), the Hinge; Brink and others, *Pattern Recognition* 45 (2012),
    162–171, the Quill, PDF: <https://www.ai.rug.nl/~mbulacu/brink-quill-pr2012.pdf>; and Junclets
    (2015), COLD (2017), FragNet (2020) and a survey, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05036>. Bear on ink
    width against stroke direction, which the planner took as a check of a stiff nib; the tool's
    `nibfit` is that check. Not marked.
30. **Dershowitz and Wolf, "Computerized Paleography: Tools for Historical
    Manuscripts"**, ICIP 2011, DOI 10.1109/ICIP.2011.6116481. Not marked; closed access.
31. **Berat Kurar-Barakat and others, VML-HP**, ICDAR 2021, PDF:
    <https://beratkurar.github.io/papers/icdar2021VmlHp.pdf>. Bears on Hebrew script types by page.
    Not read (a PDF).
32. **Trajectory recovery:** the 2010 survey by Nguyen and Blumenstein (not read); Bhunia and
    others (2018); TRACE, ICDAR 2021, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11559v1>; Díaz and others (2021);
    Hanif and Latecki, ICPRAM 2023, <https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2023/117001>. Bear on
    recovering the order of a pen's path from its ink. Not marked, except as noted.
33. **Berio and others, StrokeStyles**, *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 2022, DOI
    10.1145/3505246; record: <https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/31944>; and **CCSE**,
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13826v1>. Bear on splitting a glyph into strokes. Not marked.
34. **From memory, not checked:** Cheng-Lin Liu, In-Jung Kim and Jin H. Kim, on model-based stroke
    extraction for Chinese characters, *Pattern Recognition* 34 (2001); Itay Bar-Yosef and others,
    on recognizing degraded historical Hebrew characters, *Pattern Recognition* 42 (2009); and
    Zhouhui Lian, Bo Zhao and Jianguo Xiao, EasyFont, *ACM Transactions on Graphics* (2018). The
    ductus research named these from memory as candidates for fitting a letter model to ink.
35. **From the planner's second sub-agent, on sub-letter annotation:** the GRAPHEM project's
    article, <https://journal.digitalmedievalist.org/article/id/6984>; KIT's VISAPP paper of 2025
    on a dataset of Hebrew letters from Torah scrolls,
    <https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000179797>; Muharaf,
    <https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09630v2>; and a review of image vectorization,
    <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06441>. Not marked.

## Codices and fonts

36. **Freedman and others (eds), *The Leningrad Codex: A Facsimile Edition*** (Eerdmans
    and Brill, 1998); a review: <https://jbtc.org/v03/Freedman-etal-ed1998rev.html>. Bears on the
    photographs the crops come from. Not marked.
37. **Keter YG and Keter Aram Tsova**, fonts by Yoram Gnat in the Culmus project:
    <https://culmus.sourceforge.io/taamim/index.html>, with sample PDFs there; the planner's first
    sub-agent cited a copy of Keter YG's sources at <https://github.com/ibleaman/Keter-YG>. Bear on
    letter models for phase 2, from a font. Read by round 3: the Culmus page calls Keter YG a
    "Modern adaptation of Aleppo codex script" and Keter Aram Tsova "Based on Aleppo codex (10th
    century C.E.)". Round 3 downloaded both on 2026-10-31 with Ben's approval, after this list
    was first written. They are kept, unextracted and untracked, in the same working folder.
