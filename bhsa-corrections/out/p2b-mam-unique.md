## How the rows were judged

1. **Pre-filter** (`out/p2b-prefilter.txt`): every BHSA pointed key one small edit away from the row's (a dagesh or mappiq added or removed; one vowel removed, added or changed; a holam moved; a shin or sin dot changed; a maqaf or space inside the word removed; meteg and rafeh are not in the key) was looked up with its analyses. Every neighbour analysed with another lexeme, stem or tense is either MAM's own key (whose other analyses are homographs of MAM's word, such as the al/el corrections of Part 1 beside the negative) or a pointing with a vowel or dot that the BHSA's lacks (a sin dot for a shin dot, a qamats for a patah); none is spelled by the BHSA's pointing. The neighbours with another person, number, gender or suffix are likewise MAM's key or differ in such a mark; all are in `out/p2b-mam-unique.json` under `evidence`.
2. **Categories.** *another-word*: the pointing is a well-formed spelling of another word attested in the BHSA, and the analysis departs from it. *no-word*: the pointing spells no word (a vowel, dagesh, mappiq, furtive patah, final kaf's sheva or shin dot missing, extra, changed or misplaced) and spells no other word; the analysis reads through it. *variant*: a legitimate variation of the same word: a hataf for a sheva or the reverse; a hataf for a full vowel of the same quality or the reverse (including a hataf before a sheva, a pattern the BHSA has in 23 words); a dagesh forte omitted from, or kept in, a letter with sheva; a word-initial dagesh lene that depends on the word before; a conjunctive dagesh; a pausal or maqaf vowel; a compound name divided. *unsure*: none of these fits.
3. **Analysis column**: the BHSA's lexeme (`lex`), part of speech (`sp`), stem and tense (`vs`, `vt`), person, number and gender, state, suffix (`prs`) and gloss. MAM's form is its piece of the atom as Test B cut it, with a holam male written on the letter before the vav as the BHSA writes it; the atoms column has MAM's atom as MAM writes it. Forms are shown without accents or meteg.
