1. The answer

morphhb, the morphology of the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible, analyses the text of the Westminster Leningrad Codex, WLC 4.20. Its unit is the <w> element, called here morphhb's word: one written form between spaces or maqaf marks, which this page calls an atom, as against a chanted word, which is an atom or a whole maqaf compound. For each word morphhb gives a lemma, the dictionary word that each of its parts belongs to, as the number of its headword in Strong's Hebrew dictionary with a letter for each prefix; a morph code, or morph, which names each part's part of speech and features, such as a verb's stem, person, gender and number; and an id, a short code that names that one word, such as 01xeN.

Applying this morphology to MAM's text, Miqra According to the Masorah, is feasible. An alignment, a pairing of each of MAM's atoms with the morphhb word that corresponds to it, found by comparing their letters, gives 99.64% of MAM's 306,525 atoms morphhb's lemma, morph and id with evidence that they fit: either the atom has the same form as its morphhb word, or morphhb gives MAM's form that same lemma and morph elsewhere. The other 1,091 atoms (0.36%) need a rule or a person. The cost is an alignment that takes about 20 seconds to run, a review queue of the 384 flagged and structural atoms, and rules, or a reader, for the 707 untested ones.

2. How the comparison was made

For each verse, py/morphhb_on_mam/align.py lines up morphhb's words with MAM's atoms by their letters, once on the ketiv side of the verse and once on its qere side, as the 2026-11-13 study's “Method 2” did (its README): Python's difflib finds the longest runs of atoms whose letters agree, so that a word that one text lacks or divides differently leaves a block of atoms unpaired. On the ketiv side a ketiv stands in its place, on the qere side its qere, and the plain atoms are on both sides; an atom on both sides is counted once, so that each of MAM's atoms is counted once.

The vowel key of a form keeps its letters, its vowels, a dagesh or mappiq, and the shin and sin dots. It ignores the accents, meteg, rafeh, the upper and lower dots and invisible characters, and it reads a qamats qatan as a qamats and a ḥolam ḥaser as a ḥolam. Each of MAM's atoms gets a class, which the output files name:

  • A1: one morphhb word with the same letters and the same vowel key;
  • A2: one morphhb word with the same letters and another vowel key;
  • K: an unpointed ketiv of MAM's with the letters of one morphhb word, morphhb's ketiv, which is unpointed too, or, in a few verses, a plain word;
  • B: one morphhb word whose letters differ;
  • C: no one-to-one partner, in a block of several atoms or words, or with no partner at all;
  • AX: the same letters, but a different morphhb word on the ketiv side and on the qere side.

Where the forms differ (A2 and B), the test of support counts how morphhb analyses MAM's exact form, by the vowel key, everywhere else in its text, its ketiv and qere words included. If any of those occurrences has this word's lemma and morph, MAM's atom is supported; if MAM's form occurs but never with that lemma and morph, it is flagged; if MAM's form never occurs, it is untested. The test compares a lemma by its Strong's number alone, without its prefixes or the letter that marks one of several entries under one number.

Every Hebrew form on this page was copied from the outputs by py/morphhb_on_mam/build_page.py and is shown in MAM-normal mark order, the order that MAM-basics gives the marks of a letter, whatever order its source has; the vowel key sorts each letter's marks, so the comparison does not depend on their order. The inputs are morphhb's wlc/ at db6c5c9b, whose files are identical to those at d4265dfa, and MAM-simple's JSON in the BHS versification at MAM-basics' 374d9436.

What the vowel key could hide

The vowel key reads a qamats qatan as a qamats, so it counts 7,389 atoms as the same form although MAM's has a qamats qatan where morphhb's has a qamats (morphhb-on-mam/out/22-refine.txt, section 2); its other fold, of ḥolam ḥaser into ḥolam, hides no difference. Almost all of these qamats qatan vowels are where one is expected: 5,691 on common nouns, 429 on Qal wayyiqtol forms, 381 on proper nouns, 355 on Qal infinitives construct and 146 on Qal imperatives. In 52 of them the piece's morph is a perfect or a participle; the first 40 are listed in morphhb-on-mam/out/23-lists.json, “qatan with a perfect or participle morph”, and shown in morphhb-on-mam/out/28-show-qatan.txt. Most of these, among them 35 Hophal and 5 Pual forms, are forms that have a qamats qatan, but in Claude's first reading two look like real differences. These two belong in the review queue, although the vowel key counts them as the same form.

4. Morpheme division

morphhb divides a word into its morphemes with slashes, as between a prefix and the rest of the word, and its morph has one part for each piece between them. Carrying morphhb's analysis over means carrying its slashes into MAM's atom. Every one of morphhb's slashes stands directly before a letter, so in a pair whose letters are equal a slash goes before the same letter of MAM's atom, counted from its start: 163,726 slashes in 145,825 words (morphhb-on-mam/out/30-figures.json). In all 306,229 matched words the morph has exactly one part for each piece.

In the spelling pairs (class B) the letters themselves differ, so a slash is placed by aligning the two words' letters: 549 of their 650 slashes are placed that way (morphhb-on-mam/out/20-coverage.txt, section 10). 5. MAM's markup

Ketiv and qere

MAM's ketiv is unpointed, as morphhb's is, so a ketiv is compared by its letters: 1,018 of MAM's ketiv atoms have the letters of morphhb's ketiv and take its analysis as it stands (morphhb-on-mam/out/20-coverage.txt, section 3).

Where MAM has one form and morphhb has a ketiv and a qere, MAM's form needs one analysis, which morphhb's ketiv and qere may not share. MAM's one form is of two kinds: a trivial ketiv and qere (MAM-simple's kq-trivial), or a plain atom. In the 199 cases with one ketiv and one qere in morphhb, MAM's form has the ketiv's letters in 189 (morphhb-on-mam/out/26-kq-ids-slashes.txt, section 1).

Closed dispatch

MAM-simple's reader, py/morphhb_on_mam/mam_reader.py, handles each of MAM-simple's element types by name and stops with an error on any other, so that nothing in MAM's markup is skipped unnoticed. It found three things that a generator would have to handle:

  • 55 ketiv atoms that MAM-simple writes straight before the next atom, with no separator, where the qere ends in a maqaf, for example Numbers 23:13; the reader ends the ketiv atom there and flags it (morphhb-on-mam/out/01-mam-census.txt);
  • an empty target of a scroll-difference note at Exodus 26:6, a note about a place rather than a word;
  • a legarmeh inside an atom with a large letter at Ruth 3:13, which the reader keeps with that atom.

A product's generator would dispatch as MAM-morphhb's generator already does. py/main_mam_morphhb.py renders MAM-simple's JSON through a closed dispatch on its (type, class) pairs: morphhb_inline.render raises on an unknown pair, and its handlers, with the four structural types, cover exactly the 37 pairs present. It passes each template it reads from MAM-parsed's plus/ through validate_current_plus_template. An alignment step would read the same projection of MAM-simple, as this study's reader does.

6. Upkeep: following MAM's refreshes

MAM's text changes with each refresh from Hebrew Wikisource. MAM-basics' AGENTS.md says that a product derived from MAM's text takes each refresh through the mam-wikisource-refresh runbook, which predicts every change verse by verse and checks that those changes, and only they, appear. Such a product gets no hashes, fingerprints or pinned populations of its inputs. Its deterministic checks are closed dispatch, or a check that a hand-made statement about the data still holds, covering exactly what the statement depends on.

An alignment fits that rule in three parts.

  1. Regenerate it from scratch. The alignment is a deterministic function of MAM-simple and of one pinned commit of morphhb, and it takes about 20 seconds per strand. Nothing about MAM's text is stored beyond the product itself, so a refresh's effect on the analyses is simply the product's diff, which the runbook already has an agent predict and judge.
  2. Store each person's decision as a statement, and check it. A decision (a flagged atom's analysis, the morph of a one-form ketiv/qere, a slash that letters cannot place, a new id) is a record that names the verse, the morphhb id or ids, and MAM's whole form, letters and every mark. The build stops when MAM's form under a record changes, or when a record is not used. That is how near-Aleppo's stored pointed ketiv records already work in MAM-basics (py/near_aleppo/frozen_ketiv.py): the check covers exactly what the decision depends on, and nothing else.
  3. Move morphhb deliberately. morphhb is pinned to a commit, as this study pins db6c5c9b, and moved forward as a separate, reviewed change. Its ids are stable: the research sub-agent found Daniel's ids unchanged since they were made in 2019, and six books' ids identical to their 2022 versions.

How much a refresh would touch, from the research sub-agent's census of the 52 Wikisource downloads committed from 2026-03-12 to 2026-11-10 (morphhb-on-mam/research/upkeep-shape/FINDINGS.md, section C):

  • A download changed a median of 3.5 verses, and a median of 1 verse in its text, its ketiv/qere or an alternative form; 22 downloads changed none.
  • Over the whole period, letters changed in 3 verses, no atom was split or joined, and one vowel point changed (a qamats became a qamats qatan, at 2 Kings 22:1). Most changes were to meteg marks (134 verses), accents and documentation notes, which leave an atom's class unchanged, because the comparison's vowel key ignores them.
  • Changes to ketiv/qere structure came to 192 verse changes, mostly two template migrations. A migration of that kind would change the structure the alignment reads, and its effect would show in the product's diff.
  • Since 2026-09-16, 19 downloads changed the text or the ketiv/qere of 12 verses, 6 atoms in all.

MAM-morphhb's generator is hand-run, outside the mega, and by Ben's decision of 2026-11-03 a refresh of MAM's text does not oblige rerunning it, so it may lag MAM-simple. A morphology layer would be an input of that generator, so a change to the layer would oblige a rerun; whether the layer should instead follow every refresh is a decision for Ben.

7. Product shape and upstream

Where the result could live

MAM-morphhb is the product that already gives MAM in morphhb's format. It is distributed data, built by py/main_mam_morphhb.py from MAM-simple's BHS-numbered JSON, with titles, parashot and poetic line breaks from MAM-parsed's plus/, and checked against the OSIS 2.1.1 schema. Its MAPM.xml has verse milestones and no <w> element. It writes each of the 1,047 ketiv/qere pairs as a ketiv seg and a qere seg, MAM's 149 trivial pairs as one pointed form, and only the cant-combined strand of a dual cantillation. Its line layout serves OpenScriptorium's importer, which reads the file line by line.

Three shapes are possible.

  1. Inside MAM-morphhb: MAPM.xml gains <w lemma morph id>, as morphhb's wlc/ files have. morphhb's users would find the format familiar. But OSIS does not allow a lowercase id attribute (openscriptures/morphhb#84), so the file would lose the OSIS 2.1.1 validity that the review of openscriptures/morphhb#114 counted in its favour; whether OpenScriptorium's importer would still read it was not examined. The one strand that MAPM.xml writes is enough for the analyses, since every atom's analysis is the same in all three strands.
  2. A new table, declared in py/product_scopes.py: one row per MAM atom, keyed by verse and atom index, carrying MAM's form as a guard, morphhb's lemma, morph and id, and how the match was made. That is the shape of MAM-basics' per-word precedents: Phonetic MAM keys each chanted word by verse and row order, and near-Aleppo keys its records by verse and index with the form as a guard. No MAM-basics product has stable word ids; morphhb's would be the first.
  3. Both: the table as the record, and MAPM.xml's words generated from it.

What morphhb says and does

From the research sub-agent's reading of morphhb at db6c5c9b and of its issues and pull requests (morphhb-on-mam/research/upstream/FINDINGS.md):

  • morphhb's README.md (line 23), in a section Andy Hubert wrote in 2019 and Jesse Griffin merged, says the ids are unique and immutable and that another codex could reuse them where its words align with WLC's, creating new ones for new words.
  • The maintainers describe MAPM/ as a separate text kept for comparison. David Troidl wrote in 2021 that it is "a completely different project" (openscriptures/morphhb#75), and Jesse Griffin, on 2026-11-11, that "That file is a companion text" (openscriptures/morphhb#116); morphhb's version numbers describe the morphology in wlc/.
  • Nobody has asked for an analysed MAPM/, or for morphology on another text, in 118 issues and pull requests, 396 comments and 328 commits.
  • The validator that openscriptures/morphhb#115 added checks words only in wlc/: each id must match [0-9]{2}[A-Za-z0-9]{3} and be unique across the books, each morph must have the right shape, and each lemma must be present. MAPM/MAPM.xml gets only milestone, text and verse-inventory checks, so an analysed file in today's one-file form would pass with its words unchecked. Checking them would need a rule of MAPM's own for ids, since reused ids would collide with wlc/'s.
  • An id is the book's two-digit number in the KJV's order of books followed by three characters from a 58-character alphabet. All 306,785 of morphhb's ids are distinct, but the three characters repeat from book to book. morphhb has no tool for making new ids: the generator, in openscriptures/morphhb-parsing, has been disabled since 2019-01-13. Of the possible three-character strings, 173,136 are used in no book.
  • In practice one maintainer, Jesse Griffin, reviews and merges. He merged openscriptures/morphhb#114 6.6 days after it was opened and openscriptures/morphhb#115 3.4 days after.

The questions these facts raise for Ben are on the decision page, a private artifact at https://claude.ai/artifact/6cWmkc1DXdxMcr797YU4TX, whose source is morphhb-on-mam/decisions.html.

8. What this study could not establish

  • Whether the analyses of the 707 untested atoms fit MAM's forms: morphhb has no other occurrence of those forms to test them against.
  • Whether each flagged atom is a real change: the judgments on this page are Claude's first reading only.
  • Analyses that turn on an accent, beyond the stress screen of section 3.
  • Anything about the Leningrad Codex itself: the comparison is of two transcriptions, and no image of the manuscript was consulted.
  • Whether morphhb's maintainers want an analysed MAPM, the copy of MAM's text in morphhb's MAPM/ folder: the only evidence is documentary.
  • MACULA Hebrew's syntax and senses, which ride on the same ids: they were not studied.
  • The hours that a person's review of the queue would take.
