What these methods cannot find

The report judged 195 words: 161 that its methods found and 34 more that its brief, the request that it answered, named. The verdicts here also judge the 146 words that its general test listed as too rare for it to judge. Its methods:

  1. A general test of lemmas and morphs. It aligned morphhb's atoms with MAM's by letters, ketiv side with ketiv side and qere side with qere side, and took the 7,757 one-to-one pairs whose letters agree and whose forms differ once accents, meteg and rafeh are set aside (6,466 of them differ only by a ḥolam in MAM's form, 6,459 of those in the Tetragrammaton), and the 1,017 pairs whose letters differ. It flags a word when its lemma, its morph or both are common among the other occurrences of MAM's form (at least 50%) and rare among those of its own form (at most 10% of at least 5). For the 865 pairs whose own form is rarer, two further tests do the same with other occurrences in place of its own form's: those of the other forms with the same letters, and those of morphhb's words where MAM has the word's form.
  2. A census of morphhb's 4,930 words whose last morpheme has the letters alef and lamed.
  3. WLC's notes: the 59 chanted words under note U whose forms differ from MAM's.
  4. The report's reading of 13 pairs that no test flags.

Six kinds of place are beyond these methods:

  1. A word whose form in WLC 4.20 and in MAM is the same: the methods compare the two.
  2. A difference in accents, meteg or rafeh, which the general test sets aside.
  3. A word whose own form is too rare for the general test, or one of the 49 pairs whose form in MAM never occurs in morphhb, unless another method found it.
  4. A letter difference outside a one-to-one block of the alignment.
  5. A ketiv analysed by its qere: at Isaiah 52:5 the ketiv has the qere's lemma, while 205 other ketiv words have their own.
  6. Anything about the Leningrad Codex itself: no image was consulted.
