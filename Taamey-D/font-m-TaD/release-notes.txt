Taamey D 1.1

These notes list the changes since Taamey D 1.0, published on
2026-10-26. Each release replaces this file with its own notes.

How marks below a letter are placed

- Every below mark now hangs off the below mark before it, whatever
  kind either mark is, with the gap the font already leaves between
  two below accents. So an accent encoded before a vowel, a second
  vowel on a letter, and any two below accents are placed side by
  side without a rule for the pair. Before, an accent could hang off
  a vowel, off six of the accents or off a meteg, and a vowel only
  off an atnaḥ or a meteg; a pair the font had no rule for overlapped
  at the letter's centre.
- A pair whose first mark is an accent is centred under its letter,
  as a pair whose first mark is a vowel already was. Two atoms of the
  font's corpus (an atom is the letters between two spaces or maqaf
  marks) have such a pair: an atnaḥ before a ḥiriq, held in that order
  by a combining grapheme joiner, at Numbers 7:60 and at Job 32:6.
  Their atnaḥ and ḥiriq now sit 150 units further right.
- A second vowel on a letter again sits beside the first, as it did in
  Taamey D 0.921. In 0.931 and 1.0 its ink overlapped the first
  vowel's.
- Every combining grapheme joiner (CGJ, U+034F) is now removed during
  shaping, so the mark order it holds is placed whatever marks it
  stands between. Before, the joiner was removed only between five
  pairs of marks, and elsewhere it could leave the mark after it
  unplaced or block a letter's contextual form.
- A zero-width joiner (U+200D) is still not removed. Between a ḥataf
  vowel and a meteg it still selects the form with the meteg inside
  the ḥataf; elsewhere the font leaves it alone.
- A deḥi or a yetiv, which sits at its letter's right edge, is not
  covered: it keeps its placement, and another below mark on the same
  letter can still collide with it.

Other changes

- The version number is now 1.1, and the font's unique-ID name ends
  with it.
- No glyph or character is added or removed, no advance width or
  vertical metric changes, so line heights stay the same, and no
  glyph's outline changes.
