Change qadma over tsadi to pashta.
A pashta is a post-fix accent. When two post-fix accents occur in the same word the first post-fix accent is called an "accent helper" because it shows where the accent is actually to be placed in the word. The shift from a qadma accent to a pasha accent requires a leftward shift of the symbol by one half of the letter's width. It's NOT perceptible with some fonts.
BHL has a distinct pashta over the tsadi. BHLA has no entry for this verse.

Change pashta over sin to qadma.
This Decalogue text contains double accent, the text is also in poor condition. The mark in question is a broken line coming out of the rightmost leg of the sin. A pashta is post-positive and appears on the left side of the letter. One or two pashtas can occur on a word. If a word has a single pashta, it must occur on the final letter of the word. If a word has two pashtas, one occurs on the final letter and the other acts as an "accent helper" placed over the accented letter. This word has a single, pashta/qadma-like mark over the sin and no corresponding mark on the final letter. Therefore, the mark in question isn't a pashta, but a qadma,

Note unusual and poorly placed upper dot on zaqef-qatan over vav. Add note 't'.
At question is the reddish upper dot of an apparent zaqef-qatan over the yod/vav. Two approaches are available, the first assumes the dot was once deliberately placed by the scribe. The first approach says that the reddish dot is probably the upper dot of a zaqaf-qatan over the yod displaced because of interference from a merkha from a word, סַלֵּ֥י, on the previous line. The second approach says the reddish dot is an anomaly unintended by the scribe, leaving a single dot over both the yod and the vav. A single dot over the yod would need to be a revia and second dot on the vav would be a holam. This transcription prefers the first approach because of a lack of other reddish dots in the sheet containing the text and the fact the dot is well-placed and well-sized. Also, the second approach's dot over the yod has no outstanding revia characteristics, such as a diamond shape. A note 't' is added, however.
